1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In our clinical simulation training, medical students and doctors interact with 
an AI Virtual Patient (such as Robert Chen experiencing an acute heart attack).

During these conversations, doctors do many different things:
• Ask questions (e.g., "What medications do you take?")
• Give medical instructions (e.g., "Take a Disprin tablet")
• Provide lifestyle advice (e.g., "Reduce caffeine and get regular sleep")
• Offer reassurance and breathing guidance (e.g., "Take deep breaths and relax")
• Share clinical opinions (e.g., "Maybe you took your pills on an empty stomach")

WHAT WAS THE PROBLEM?
Previously, the AI patient got confused by individual keywords. If the doctor 
said "Take Disprin" or "Reduce caffeine", the AI patient thought the doctor was 
asking a question about their past history. As a result, the patient would 
start reciting their regular prescriptions or unprompted smoking/alcohol history.

WHAT WAS FIXED?
We upgraded the AI Patient to understand the DOCTOR'S INTENT and conversational 
role before answering. Now:
1. When a doctor gives advice, the patient acknowledges it.
2. When a doctor gives a medicine, the patient agrees to take it.
3. When a doctor reassures the patient, the patient tries to relax.
4. The patient ONLY recites past medical records when the doctor actually asks for them.

2. BEFORE VS AFTER COMPARISONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SCENARIO 1: DOCTOR GIVES BREATHING / RELAXATION INSTRUCTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Doctor: "Deep breath relaxation for 5 to 10 minutes."

• BEFORE (Wrong): 
  Patient: "I'm sorry doctor, I didn't quite catch what you said..."

• AFTER (Fixed): 
  Patient: "I'll try to take deep breaths and relax for a few minutes, doctor. 
            Is that going to help ease this pressure in my chest?"


SCENARIO 2: DOCTOR GIVES LIFESTYLE & DIET ADVICE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Doctor: "Regular meals and sleep, reduce caffeine, energy drinks, and light exercise."

• BEFORE (Wrong): 
  Patient recited their smoking record: "Smokes 0.5 packs per day for 25 years. 
  Drinks 1-2 glasses of wine on weekends..."

• AFTER (Fixed): 
  Patient: "I understand, doctor. I'll make sure to cut down on caffeine and 
            energy drinks, get regular meals and sleep, and do light exercise 
            once this severe chest pain is taken care of."


SCENARIO 3: DOCTOR PRESCRIBES AN EMERGENCY TABLET (DISPRIN)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Doctor: "You can take a Disprin tablet."

• BEFORE (Wrong): 
  Patient recited their daily baseline drugs: "I take my daily medications: 
  Amlodipine 5 mg daily, Atorvastatin 20 mg daily."

• AFTER (Fixed): 
  Patient: "Okay doctor, I'll take the Disprin. Will that help relieve this 
            crushing pressure in my chest?"


SCENARIO 4: DOCTOR MAKES A CLINICAL CLAIM / OPINION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Doctor: "You take in high volume of medicine that's why you get anxiety."

• BEFORE (Wrong): 
  Patient: "I take my daily medications: Amlodipine 5 mg daily, Atorvastatin 20 mg..."

• AFTER (Fixed): 
  Patient: "I only take the daily medications my doctor prescribed for my blood 
            pressure and cholesterol, doctor. I don't know if they could cause 
            anxiety, but this crushing chest pressure feels very real and frightening."


SCENARIO 5: DOCTOR ASKS A MULTI-PART QUESTION (MEDICINE + BREAKFAST TIMING)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Doctor: "Before taking the medicine, had you breakfast?"

• AFTER (Accurate & Case-Grounded): 
  Patient: "I take my daily morning medications with water, but I was in such 
            a rush to get into the office that I don't recall having breakfast 
            before taking them this time."
